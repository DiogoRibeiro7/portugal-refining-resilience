# Parked: the Italian ISAB reading of the price placebo

**Dated 2026-08-22. Parked, not rejected — but do not revive it without re-reading the
timing objection below, which is what killed it the first time.**

## The idea

The cross-country price placebo compares Portugal against France, Italy, Germany and
Belgium at a May 2021 break. Italy comes out at a speed ratio of 4.17 (*p* = 0.064),
close to Portugal's 4.24, where the other three show nothing or move the other way.

The proposal was to reclassify Italy as a second treated case rather than a failed
control, on the grounds that ISAB at Priolo Gargallo is a refinery of national
significance that the Italian state intervened to keep operating. That would turn the
one awkward number in the placebo into supporting evidence: the same mechanism —
a single-site supply disruption at a nationally significant refinery — recovered in a
second country.

## Why it was parked

**The dates do not line up.** Every documented element of the ISAB episode postdates the
break being tested by more than a year:

| event | date |
|---|---|
| break imposed in the placebo | May 2021 |
| coordination table for ISAB business continuity (D.L. 50/2022, art. 15) | mid-2022 |
| D.L. 187/2022 trusteeship, converted by Legge 10/2023 | December 2022 |
| DPCM declaring the ISAB plants of national strategic interest | 4 February 2023 |
| EU seaborne crude embargo (Regulation 2022/879) | 5 December 2022 |

**And the data agrees with the dates.** Splitting the post-closure period at the embargo
(now reported in the paper) puts the Italian change in the later window, not the earlier
one:

| pair | before closure | closure → embargo | embargo onward |
|---|---|---|---|
| PT–ES | −0.1415 | **−0.8294** (*p* < 0.001) | −0.5032 |
| IT–ES | −0.0258 | −0.0562 (*p* = 0.240) | −0.2443 (*p* = 0.020) |
| BE–ES | −0.4225 | −0.3483 | −0.6223 (interaction *p* = 0.028) |

Italy shows nothing in the window that isolates the Portuguese closure. Its change
arrives with the embargo, alongside Belgium's. Read at a December 2022 break alone,
France (2.78) and Belgium (1.50) move as well, so that date produces common movement
across countries.

So the Italian result belongs to the embargo response. The ISAB chain does not account
for a change dated to May 2021, because the ISAB events had not happened yet.

## The methodological trap this avoided

Reclassifying a control as treated *after* seeing that it came out at 4.17 is a post-hoc
move, and a referee would say so regardless of whether the reclassification happened to
be right. The defensible order is to state the classification rule first, then apply it.
Doing that here showed the reclassification was wrong on the facts — not merely risky as
procedure.

## What is citable, if this is ever revived

These were verified and are archival. They are **not** currently cited anywhere, because
the paper no longer makes the claim they would support.

- Legge 1 febbraio 2023, n. 10, converting decreto-legge 5 dicembre 2022, n. 187,
  *misure urgenti a tutela dell'interesse nazionale nei settori produttivi strategici*;
  Gazzetta Ufficiale n. 28, 3 February 2023. Article 1 obliges undertakings in
  hydrocarbon refining managing activities of strategic national importance to guarantee
  security of supply and production continuity. Camera dossier at `temi.camera.it`.
- D.L. 50/2022, art. 15, comma 13-bis — coordination table for ISAB S.r.l. business
  continuity at Priolo Gargallo. Places the intervention in mid-2022.
- DPCM 4 February 2023 — declares the ISAB plants of national strategic interest under
  decreto-legge 207/2012.
- Council Regulation (EU) 2022/879 — the embargo dates. This one **is** cited, for the
  date of the common shock only.

Capacity share (ISAB is roughly a fifth of Italian refining) should be taken from
FuelsEurope's *Statistical Report 2025*, already in `references.bib`, rather than from
press coverage.

**Do not use** the claim that ISAB relied solely on Russian crude from February 2022.
That is press-sourced only. The legal instruments establish that the state judged
continuity and supply security to be at risk at a strategically significant site, which
is all the argument would need.
